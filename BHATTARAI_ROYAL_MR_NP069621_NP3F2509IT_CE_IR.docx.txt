Abstract
This document provides an overview of the development for PricePilot which is a cloud-based mobile app aiming to enhance online shopping in Nepal. The Nepali online marketplace is currently very geographically and fragmentally dispersed. Therefore, shoppers have difficulty finding accurate information on price, discount, etc. To better understand the issues with online shopping in Nepal and what user expectations will be for the app, we undertook a structured survey focusing on Nepali consumer's shopping behavior, problems, and requirements. PricePilot aims to meet the needs of its users through an innovative price scrapping and normalization system across multiple platforms. In addition, PricePilot contains a module that calculates a Deal Score, this helps identify whether or not an offer made by an on-line retailer is legitimate. The product was created using a modular architecture, which includes a FastAPI back end, a React Native mobile UI, and cloud-based databases.

Chapter 1: Introduction
Background to the Project 
Shopping online in Nepal has become increasingly popular within the last decade. The use of smartphones is increasing annually (Bhattarai & Thapa, 2024). There is a decrease in the cost of internet connectivity. Electronic payment options such as eSewa and Khalti handle thousands of transactions per month (Parajuli et al., 2025). E-commerce sites are attracting an increasing number of shoppers who are looking to buy electronics and household items (Shrestha et al., 2025). The figure below clearly shows that the number of e-commerce users in Nepal has grown from nearly 1 million to 4 million in a couple of years, and it seems to be only going up.
Figure 1Growth of E-Commerce Users in Nepal
Note. From E-commerce in Nepal: Growth, opportunities, and threats, by D. Chapagain and B. Aryal, 2024, Preprints.  https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202410.1585.v1
Despite the development, there is still fragmentation in the marketplace. Every platform works on its own, maintains its own database of products, and has its own price without disclosing any of its data to other platforms (Khadka et al., 2025). In case a customer wants to purchase a laptop, he/she will browse one platform first, and afterward opens another platform in a new tab, and may go to another one again. All the platforms offer the same product but at different prices. No platform provides competitive information on its web site. This means that buyers must invest their time or money (Shrestha S. K., 2023).
Nepal-specific research supports this behavior in online consumer decision-making and pricing practices. Studies show that price strongly influences online purchasing decisions. This is claimed based on data collected from 400 respondents in Kathmandu Valley (Manandhar & Chalise, 2023). However, the fragmented structure of the e-commerce market limits effective price comparison and forces consumers to rely on incomplete information. A separate survey of 202 Nepalese youth has ranked ease of price comparison as the most important feature, clearly showing strong demand for comparison tools (Shrestha et al., The Digital Consumer Economy: Purchasing Behavior of Youth in E-Commerce Markets, 2025). Still, the market still lacks adequate platforms to meet this need. Researchers also observe that prices on major Nepali e-commerce platforms fluctuate between 5% and 11% (Rahman, 2025). They further found a strong positive correlation between price variation and platform revenue. This suggests that sellers benefit from reduced price transparency. As a result, sellers often increase the prices before promotional periods and later reduce them during sales, creating the persona of discounts without offering real savings.
PricePilot solves all these problems directly. PricePilot functions as a web-based price comparison platform made for the Nepalese e-commerce sites. The system extracts the live prices by means of web scraping. It also maintains the records of the price trends in the past to come up with a Deal Score, which allows the consumer to decide if the discount offered is legitimate or just an inflated reference price.
Problem Statement
In the last ten years, there have been remarkable changes in the digital market of Nepal, moving from a conventional market to an online market because people use smartphones and inexpensive internet facilities (Thakur, 2025). In the old days, the buying experience was simple. People would visit the shop, ask about the price, and bargain at times. Nevertheless, the rise of the online market does not mean having a single market. It means fragmenting the market into many small and medium-scale firms operating independently and marketing their products for distinct customer groups (Rukhmana & Dwiarta, 2026). However, the presence of many stores is not the main issue. The real problem lies in the absence of a system that connects these stores and enables efficient price discovery for consumers (Devkota et al., 2021).
The consumer may notice that the same product is available on several different platforms when looking to purchase a particular product. Each of these platforms has a different name to the product, sets a price for it, and does not provide any means by which to verify that each listing refers to the same product. There is no API used between these platforms to share information about products since they operate independently as different platforms (Chen, 2024). Platforms generally rely on manual updates of product data, meaning that the prices shown do not represent current market conditions (Shaikh et al., 2023).
This problem can be easily understood by considering an example of the Rs. 45,000 laptop case. Suppose that a laptop is sold for Rs. 45,000. Before the sale period, the price of the laptop is raised to Rs. 60,000. Then during the Sale period, the price is again brought down to Rs. 45,000. However, now the seller claims a discount of 25% is given for the product. The customer thinks that they benefitted from the discount, but they had to pay the same price because there is no change in the price. This is because the customer does not have any information about the previous price.
Figure 2Consumer Belief of Fake Discounts from Businesses
Note. From Deceptive deals or real savings? 51% of consumers say brands regularly float fake discounts, by YouGov, 2024. https://yougov.com/articles/49460-deceptive-deals-or-real-savings-51-of-consers-say-brands-regularly-float-fake-discounts.
The key problems that this project addresses are described below:
Fragile Market with High Cost of Search: The e-commerce market in Nepal does not have a centralized platform that links the various retail portals. The consumers need to go through each platform separately, look for the same product with different naming standards, and then make comparisons between them. Studies reveal that such a fragmented approach makes the consumers to give up their search, leading to overpayment for products (Devkota et al., 2021).
Manipulating Prices through False Discounts: Recent studies reveal that the pricing mechanism has an effect on the decision-making process of consumers in e-commerce portals of Nepal (Rahman, 2025). It has also been found that there is an inverse relationship between price discrimination and competitiveness. This suggests that the lack of transparency in prices along with the variation in prices reduces the decision-making capacity of consumers. On the other hand, there is a strong influence of discounts on the decision-making process of consumers.
Lack of Tools for the Local Market: Currently available global shopping price comparison tools are all based on standard retailing systems and voluntary participation by retailers (Fadhilla et al., 2024). No current tool facilitates shopping price comparison for local shopping sites in Nepal. Hence, the local gaps remain unaddressed.
Technical Isolation of Local Platforms: While international markets for online shopping have the advantage of standardized data exchange through API interfaces between retailers and third parties, Nepal’s local retail portals suffer from technical isolation. These portals do not provide any product feeds or APIs. So, any scraper platform would be required to perform independent web scraping to obtain data on prices. However, this is not a drawback but rather part of the challenge being solved.
Rationale
The evolution of PricePilot is based on a market gap which was found by conducting primary and secondary research. There is no solution in the Nepalese online shopping market which provides price aggregation in real-time. This is from local platforms, historic price fluctuations and the genuineness of price reductions. Moreover, there is no mobile platform for consumers having basic knowledge of technology. The current market lacks such kind of platform. Global price comparison software works in environments where there is uniformity of infrastructure and robust API integration. Such an environment is not available in Nepal. Consequently, such software will not provide relevant information in the local environment. The problem does not come from the incapability of the technology but from its non-availability in the local environment. The issue also has an ethical angle. The seller has full information regarding the pricing process, including historical prices, while the consumer does not have access to this information. The information asymmetry here works against the consumer, particularly the inexperienced one. PricePilot addresses this issue by offering information on the history of prices and discounts. This gives power back to the consumer and facilitates fair competition. The use of cloud-native practice's during scraping of data will be used in this project. This consists of scheduling tasks, scraping data off of the websites, and managing the errors.
Project Aim
To create a smart cloud-based price comparison system that can aggregate, analyze, and visualize real-time product price information from various category-specific e-commerce platform in the highly fragmented digital marketplace in Nepal, which can allow consumers to compare product prices, identify genuine discounts, and make purchasing decisions while saving search time and improving price transparency.
Project Objectives
To build a category-centric web scraping system to fetch real-time product information from niche platform on different categories of products.
To build an automated system to fetch prices of the products on diverse platforms and store historical data on a cloud database to analyze trends.
To build a deal score algorithm to authenticate the originality of deals by comparing concurrent price on multiple platforms.
To build a cross-platform mobile application featuring real-time price comparison, price history visualization, a unified Wishlist, price-drop alerts, and one-click merchant redirection.

Potential Benefits
Tangible Benefits
Real Time Price Comparison: Users can make comparisons on prices from different markets within one window instead of visiting each website separately.
Deal Legitimacy Check: The Deal Score algorithm verifies the authenticity of offers by comparing the real-time prices to past data that shows previous manipulation of prices before any sale took place.
Price History Visibility: Interactive price chart for 30-60 days that will help the user decide whether to buy immediately or wait until further price decline.
Automated Price Drop Alerts: User sets an ideal price level, after which a notification will be triggered automatically once price meets user's requirements.
Unified Shopping Wishlist: All saved items are stored in one list regardless of vendor's website, allowing user to find the optimal combination of platforms for lower overall expenses.
Intangible Benefits
Enhanced Consumer Confidence: Users rely on verified pricing and historical data, which increases trust and improves confidence in purchasing decisions.
Reduced Decision Fatigue: Users access all required information in one platform, which simplifies decision-making and removes the need to compare multiple sites manually.
Promotion of Transparent Market Practices: The system exposes price manipulation through historical data, which encourages fair pricing and increases market transparency.
Informed Spending Habits: Users understand price trends and avoid impulsive purchases, leading to more informed and controlled spending behavior.
Time Savings: Users save significant time by avoiding manual comparison across platforms and can focus on more productive activities.
Target Users
PricePilot targets online shoppers in Nepal who actively use local e-commerce platforms to purchase technology products but lack an effective tool to compare prices and verify discount claims. The system serves four key user groups:
Price-Conscious Online Shoppers: Regular purchasers of electronics and tech products that rely on manually comparing prices across different websites before purchasing an item.
Technology-Savvy Young Adults: Consumers aged between 20-35 who are familiar with using mobile applications and making digital payments but have no tool that can help consolidate pricing data on products before they purchase.
Frequent Online Shoppers: Consumers that purchase items from the internet multiple times per month are experiencing price search fatigue and have difficulty confirming if the discount being offered is accurate due to misrepresentation.
Budget-Sensitive Buyers: Consumers who are looking to find the lowest price available and are unable to confirm they have found the best prices available for shopping at local shopping websites.
Functionalities (Deliverables)
PricePilot aims to help consumers in Nepal via an intelligent and efficient price comparison application. It is easy for consumers to do price comparisons using different sources of information and buy their desired items. Data collection, data analysis, and data visualization all come together in creating a user-friendly platform through this application.

Registration and login services are available on the platform through the use of secure cloud-based authentication services.
Searching for products and accessing live pricing information for the products offered on various online shopping sites is possible on the application.

How PricePilot Aligns with SDG 12
PricePilot helps achieve SDG 12 through provision of verified data that allows for identification of fake discounts. This is done through verification of prices via Deal Score and tracking prices over time. It empowers customers to base their decisions on facts, not marketing.
Through PricePilot, there is also increased transparency since it reveals any manipulative behavior through the recorded price history. The increased dependence on the price history by consumers makes it difficult for sellers to manipulate consumers due to information asymmetry. Hence, the tool not only aids consumer decision-making but also ensures that the markets act responsibly and ethically.

Nature of Challenges
Every ambitious system comes with its own set of challenges which needs to be tackled in the process of development. Some of the major challenges faced by the PricePilot system are given below:
Web Scraper's Fragility and Upkeep: The web scraper depends on the website's static structure. However, Nepali e-commerce websites often change their formats, leading to the need for constant updating of scrapers.
Anti-Bot Protection Measures: To prevent scraping, websites utilize anti-bot mechanisms such as rate limiting, CAPTCHA and others. So, the system must manage them effectively using session management and controlled requests.
Product Standardization: Products are shown in different ways depending on the platform used. It includes differences in names, attributes and variations. It can prove challenging for the system to match them.
Data Validity and Reliability: The platform needs to show accurate and timely information about prices. In case of wrong information, user confidence will be lost.
Cross-Platform Mobile Development: The software needs to operate efficiently in both the Android and iOS platforms. Therefore, thorough testing needs to be done before the final deployment.

Chapter 2: Literature Review
Introduction
The issue of price comparisons within fragmented digital markets has been present since the early days of e-commerce and many studies have been conducted on topics ranging from consumer behavior and web scraping to misleading prices and algorithmic pricing mechanisms. This chapter will review some of the literature related to this topic, discussing its significance regarding PricePilot. In particular, this chapter will examine the problems that hinder price transparency within Nepal's online retail sector, which requires finding an efficient technical solution. Moreover, this chapter will assess existing online price comparison websites through features, accuracy, discount verification, and adaptability to Nepal.
Domain Research
Nepal's E-Commerce Landscape and Consumer Behavior
The e-commerce market in Nepal has been growing rapidly over the last decade, with the rise of smartphone usage and the popularity of digital payment systems like eSewa and Khalti (Rijal & Bhandari, 2026). However, the market has been disorganized, with the number of online retailers growing instead of the number of well-integrated online retail sites (Tabsum & Kumar, 2026). For instance, if an individual wants to purchase a smartphone or a laptop, they will have to visit each online retailer website, noting the price of the product, as they will not have a single source to compare price. This is not just an issue of inconvenience, as it also become costly for the buyer.
Figure 4Online Consumer Products Research Process
Note. From Consumer Product Research Statistics, by Capital One Shopping, 2025. https://capitaloneshopping.com/research/consumer-product-research-statistics/.
One of the strongest factors that determine online purchasing behavior among consumers in Nepal are price sensitivity (Phd, 2019). However, tools to help consumers make the most of this factor is not readily available in the market. Most consumers want to get the best price, but they don’t want to spend their precious time searching for it. Perceived benefits and ease of access is two major factors that determine consumers behavior in purchasing online. These two factors are affected in a negative manner due to the fragmented nature of the online market, where consumers must search multiple websites to get information about prices (Sejuwal, 2025). The difference between what consumers want and what is currently possible in the market show that there is a real need for a tool like PricePilot.
Web Scraping and Real-Time Price Aggregation
Most of the e-commerce sites in Nepal do not offer public APIs. In that case, the best option is to use web scraping (Chavan, 2024). However, different websites require different approaches to be used while scraping. For some websites, which have static web pages, simple HTTP protocol-based scrapers can be used. But today, most of the online retail sites use dynamic page generation, which is done by JavaScript. In those cases, simple scrapers will not be able to fetch the pages, as they will be empty. Headless browser automation tools are required, which can mimic the actions of real browsers and can execute JavaScript (Kuo et al., 2026). Some of the target websites of PricePilot, like Daraz, Oliz Store, and GadgetByte, use this approach. Hence, Playwright was selected as the best choice for the task, as compared to other simple scrapers.
However, extracting this data is just half the hurdle. Once this data is obtained from various sources, the same product is referred to by various names or formats. For instance, a laptop is referred to as “Lenovo IdeaPad 5” on one platform and “IdeaPad 5 Gen 8 AMD” on another. However, for any meaningful comparison to take place, the system must first understand that they are essentially talking about the same product. This process is referred to as data normalization. It is often underestimated but is one of the major hurdles in developing price comparison systems (Hadasik, 2024). PricePilot solves this problem by using a category-focused architecture with a product mapping layer suitable for Nepali retailers.
Fake Discounts and Deceptive Pricing in Online Retail
The problem of fake discounts is common in online retail, especially in the electronic products segment. Essentially, the idea behind this technique is very simple. The merchant increases the price of the product slightly before the sale, and then lowers it back to the original price once the sale starts. This is then advertised as a massive discount (Hoolikatti et al., 2025). From the buyer's perspective, it's like they're getting an amazing bargain. In fact, they have no idea whether the price was ever lowered or not. They don't have access to data regarding the price changes, so they can't verify the claim (Muktadir et al., 2025).
The lack of price transparency has been cited as one of the major causes of reduced consumer trust in digital marketplaces. It has been found that consumers who have access to price history makes more informed purchasing decisions (Hasmalawati et al., 2024). They are not easily swayed by false promotional prices. They can spot real deals. This idea is what inform the idea of the PricePilot’s ‘Deal Score’ feature. By comparing the price of a product to its price history and averages across different platforms, it can be determined whether the product is being sold at a genuine discount (Pandey et al., 2024). In this way, price history level the information advantage that sellers have over consumers.
Dynamic/Algorithmic Pricing and its Impact on Customer Confidence
The use of dynamic or algorithmic pricing have been observed in recent times in modern electronic commerce environments. In this type of pricing, the price of products is constantly updated based on different factor (Vomberg et al., 2025). Even though this type of pricing is advantageous to sellers, it can be uncertain for customers. When customers notices that the price of products is constantly changing, they may not be clear about the reasons behind the change in price (Banerjee, 2025). In this regard, price transparency tools can be used to shows customers the change in price over time. When customers have access to price history data, they can easily understand the pricing process. This will also allow customers to feel more in control of their purchase decisions.
Algorithmic pricing can also result in higher average market prices due to the use of similar algorithmic pricing strategies by various platforms (Spann et al., 2024). Although the pricing practices in Nepal are still in the manual phase, algorithmic promotions are being used during major sales. There is a lack of information between buyers and sellers. Such information gaps are not just confined to developing countries, but they are seen in various online platforms where the pricing mechanism is dynamic and complex to understand (Varnukhov, 2025). PricePilot can function in this environment by increasing the visibility of prices and helping consumers compare the prices in a better way.

Why PricePilot?
If we compare all these tools, we can clearly see that they are effective in their own domains. However, they are created with the idea of working in environments that have standardized retail infrastructure, voluntary retailer engagement, and well-established API infrastructures. None of the tools use independent data scraping, and none of the tools operate in the Nepali environment. Moreover, none of the tools allow users to check whether the discount is genuine or artificially created by inflating the price. Even though all these tools have a wide array of features, the problem of artificial discounts remains unresolved.
However, PricePilot addresses these shortcomings in three major ways. Firstly, the tool relies on web scrapers to fetch data in real-time directly from local Nepali e-commerce sites. As a result, there is no need to require retailers to register or opt-in to provide data. With this method, the tool can include retailers that may not have been included in the sites discussed above. Secondly, the tool offers users a Deal Score that helps in identifying whether the discounted prices offered are genuine or artificially manipulated. This addresses one of the shortcomings that the other tools may not have addressed. Thirdly, the tool has been tailored to suit the Nepali e-commerce environment. As a result, even though the tools discussed above can serve users in other regions, they may not offer the same service and relevance to users in Nepal, especially if they are looking to buy products such as laptops or smartphones at reasonable and competitive prices.
Technical Research
System Architecture
Figure 10PricePilot System Architecture
The architecture of PricePilot is designed with a cloud-based layer architecture, which enables searching, comparing, and discounting. The whole system starts from the Presentation Layer. In the Presentation Layer, the customer interacts with the React Native application and passes their requests to the backend through REST API and JSON Web Tokens (JWT).
Application layer controls the system logic through the FastAPI backend. The Application layer also provides other services including normalization, Deal Score calculation, and notification services. Product data, discount validation, and user services like Wish List and notifications are part of the responsibilities handled by the backend.
The Data Acquisition Layer extracts data about products from several Nepali e-commerce websites using a multi-site web scraper pipeline. The raw data is stored in MongoDB. After cleansing and normalizing, the system saves the data in PostgreSQL.
The Data Layer distinguishes structured from unstructured data. Both PostgreSQL and MongoDB are used to enhance efficiency. In most cases, the architecture provides services such as data collection, data processing, data storage, and user interaction.
System Flowchart
Figure 11Flowchart of the Proposed System
The above flowchart demonstrates the entire process involved in running PricePilot application. The procedure begins when the user searches for a product through the app and the system passes the information to the backend. Next, the system checks if the data it needs can be found in the database. In case if the data cannot be found in the database, the process of web scraping begins where the system extracts data from different sources. In case if there is no need to use the web scraping process, the system skips this step. As soon as the process is finished, the system calculates the Deal Score which helps to determine if the discount was offered for real and shows the results to the user. Afterward, the system analyzes whether the user desires to receive alerts about the price drop below the selected price level. If yes, the system will send the alert messages when the price falls below the chosen level.

Justification of Frontend Framework Chosen
Comparing the three choices, it could be said that they all have their own merits. Flutter provides good performance and cross-platform development, yet at the same time, there is a problem associated with the use of Dart. Learning Dart will take extra effort without any tangible benefits. Moreover, the libraries of Flutter are still under development, and their number is much smaller than that of React Native. This will lead to the necessity of developing more features manually. Native development on Swift and Kotlin will result in maximum performance. However, the developer will be faced with the need to develop two different codebases for Android and iOS. In other words, the workload on updates will almost double, which is hardly possible for one developer.
React Native with TypeScript resolves all of the above issues very efficiently. First of all, the need to develop two separate apps for Android and iOS is solved with React Native because one codebase can be utilized on both platforms. It saves time and minimizes the amount of work put into development. Additionally, TypeScript improves the stability of the system because data structures' errors will be caught while compiling the code, so there would not be any crashes caused by incorrect data that were extracted from the web. The ecosystem is very stable and includes lots of useful libraries implementing PricePilot's features, which provide necessary functionality for PricePilot, including visualizations, push notifications, and deep linking. Hence, this is the best possible option for this project.
Backend: FastAPI with Python
Note. From Python logo, by Python Software Foundation, n.d. https://www.python.org/community/logos/ and FastAPI logo, by Sebastián Ramírez, n.d. https://fastapi.tiangolo.com.
Python is a high-level programming language that can be used for web development, automation, data analysis, and machine learning. It was first released in 1991 by Guido van Rossum. It's one of the most sought-after programming languages due to its simplicity and availability of libraries (Tarale, 2025). In PricePilot, Python is used for the entire backend stack, including the web scraping engine to fetch information from Nepali websites, normalization of fetched information, calculation of deal score to verify the authenticity of discounts, and finally providing information in the form of REST APIs to the mobile frontend.
FastAPI is used to develop the API layer. FastAPI was developed and released in 2018 and is considered a modern and high-performance Python framework that is built on top of the Starlette and Pydantic frameworks (Ruchi et al., 2026). FastAPI was designed to develop APIs quickly and securely. FastAPI support asynchronous programming using Python’s asyncio system, which allow the server to handle multiple scraping operations and API requests simultaneously without being blocked. Additionally, it enforces strict validation using Pydantic models, which is crucial in handling scraped data, especially pricing information, which may not always be in a consistent format. FastAPI can automatically generates interactive API documentation, thus avoiding the need for using API testing tools during development.
Advantages
The entire backend pipeline, from scraping, normalization, scoring, and finally the API, is written in Python, eliminating the need to switch languages or environments
Native async support of FastAPI enables multiple scraping jobs or API requests to be processed concurrently without blocking the server
Pydantic provides strict validation of both inputs and outputs, catching invalid pricing data before it reaches the database
FastAPI automatically generates interactive API documentation, making endpoint testing fast without the need for a separate tool
Being a widely used language, Python has a strong community, meaning common problems have well-documented and readily available solutions
Disadvantages
Being interpreted, Python has the disadvantage of being slower than Go or C++ when it comes to pure throughput, although this difference is only noticeable at extremely high scraping volumes
Mixing async and sync code in the same scraping pipeline, all written in the same language, requires careful management of the async code to avoid accidentally blocking the event loop
The need to self-host the FastAPI server requires more infrastructure management than a backend service

Database Management System 
PricePilot makes use of two different database systems. Each database system plays a different role. The reason for using two different database systems instead of one was based on the nature of the information that the system works with. Some information was highly structured, while some was loosely structured. Forcing both types of information into one database system would require a rigid system that cannot accommodate the loosely structured information, or a flexible system that cannot provide the necessary integrity for user accounts and prices.
PostgreSQL
Figure 12PostgreSQL Logo
Note. From PostgreSQL logo, by PostgreSQL Global Development Group, n.d. https://www.postgresql.org/about/press/.
PostgreSQL is used as the primary relational database, and it store user accounts, saved products, price history records, Wishlist, and alert settings. It is used as it allows for ACID compliance, which ensure that the price history records are kept consistent, especially if the scraper is inserting multiple records at once (Ijaz, 2026). The process of calculating the deal score and analyzing the price trend are a complex query that compare the price of a product to its history and the price across multiple platforms, and it is handled well by PostgreSQL. The row security policies in PostgreSQL ensure that only the user can access their own records, providing an extra layer of security.
Advantages
PostgreSQL guarantees that its ACID-compliant database management system remains reliable despite high volumes of work.
It can manage highly related data through its ability to support complex queries, joins, and transactions.
The database is structured using tables and constraints, ensuring that all data remains consistent and free from any errors.
It offers some advanced functionalities including indexes, constraints, and window functions, improving performance and analysis of the data.
It offers some advanced functionalities including indexes, constraints, and window functions, improving performance and analysis of the data.
Disadvantages
The developers must determine a set schema beforehand in PostgreSQL, making it inflexible for data whose structure changes dynamically.
It is not efficient at processing unstructured data, especially in comparison to NoSQL databases.
It demands more database designing and planning time from the user, especially when it comes to establishing complex connections.
It also complicates horizontal scaling as distributing data among many servers becomes much more challenging.
MongoDB
Figure 13MongoDB Logo
Note. From MongoDB logo, by MongoDB, Inc., n.d. https://www.mongodb.com/company/brand.
MongoDB is used alongside PostgreSQL to manage the unstructured data in this setup. These include unprocessed scraped product information, results from the normalization pipeline, and cached data  (Sriram et al., 2025). Once this data is scraped from Daraz or GadgetByte by the scraper, this data is not always neatly structured. Some fields are missing, and some fields are in different positions depending on where they are scraped from. This data is stored in a document database, which is then processed by the normalization pipeline before being written to PostgreSQL. Another advantage is that since this is a horizontal scaling database, this data store will not have to change any structure as more and more products are scraped.
Advantages
It has a flexible document-oriented schema that makes it easy to manipulate data schemas as the needs of the application change.
It can manage both unstructured and semi-structured data, making it applicable in current applications characterized by changing data formats.
It can be scaled horizontally using sharding, which helps it cope with huge data volumes and traffic.
It provides excellent performance in simple data retrieval and storage applications that demand fast access to data.
It provides fast development as developers can develop their applications without being constrained by any schema.
Disadvantages
MongoDB is less consistent in some scenarios because it does not guarantee complete ACID compliance as relational databases do.
It supports fewer capabilities for complex queries and transactions involving multiple tables and relations, making it less appropriate for highly relational datasets.
It demands redundant copies of data because of its denormalized nature, thus leading to higher data storage overheads and inefficiencies.
It processes complex queries and deeply nested relationships inefficiently, particularly when compared with relational databases such as PostgreSQL.

Summary
To introduce the problem and the solution offered by PricePilot, the paper first introduces four different areas of domain research. Firstly, while Nepal's e-commerce industry grows fast, it also suffers from fragmentation that compels buyers to search for products on several websites simultaneously and to spend too much money on their purchases. For that reason, web scraping emerges as one of the best ways to gather data from websites such as Daraz, Oliz Store, and GadgetByte that have no public APIs. To deal with dynamic pages based on JavaScript, it is suggested to make use of such software as Playwright. Secondly, the problem of deceptive pricing occurs when sellers raise prices in anticipation of a sale in order to create an illusion of discounts. One should note that the availability of information about price history enables users to find good deals, thereby supporting the idea of implementing the Deal Score functionality. Lastly, the problem of algorithmic pricing and how price transparency tools can enhance the understanding of price variations is addressed.
Secondly, this section of the review will examine five price comparison services which are Google Shopping, PriceSpy, CamelCamelCamel, Idealo, and PriceRunner in terms of their capabilities like real-time price comparison, price history availability, price discount verification, and regional markets supported. Every one of these platforms is suitable for use in its respective geographic region. However, these price comparison services have identical shortcomings that include reliance on retailers to supply information, lack of support for the Nepali market, and inability to verify discounts. Following this, the next section will explain why the technical research chose the particular technologies that were selected for use in the project. The frontend was built using React Native and TypeScript, the backend was developed with FastAPI using Python, and the database system utilized two databases, namely PostgreSQL and MongoDB to store structured and raw data respectively. Visual Studio Code was chosen as the preferred development environment. These choices were backed up by considerations of other options including Flutter, Node.js, Django, PyCharm, and MySQL.
Chapter 3: System Development Methodology
Introduction
The system development methodology refers to a structured way of planning, building, and deploying an entire software system from the beginning until its final deployment to users. The methodology determines how development activities are to be carried out in various phases throughout the lifecycle  (Hossain, 2023). Therefore, it is a set of processes which cover all activities associated with gathering requirements, designing the solution, implementing it, conducting tests, and maintaining the system after deployment. To put it simply, it is the roadmap for the project development.
With a well-defined methodology, development processes remain organized and structured; consequently, the project gains flexibility in responding to problems that may emerge. Resource management is also improved by the adoption of this approach. This is crucial since unstructured development will soon lose its direction, which can result in a series of problems.
Typically, there exist two broad categories of system development methodologies, traditional and modern. While traditional approaches like Waterfall and V-Model include a linear flow from one activity to the other without backtracking, modern methodologies employ the cyclical model  (Morozov & Kulyk, 2025). Examples of modern methodologies include Agile and Spiral. The former two are characterized by strict structure while the latter two allow room for improvement through feedback.
This chapter will evaluate several system development methodologies using evaluation criteria that are relevant to the project. After identifying the best strategy, the chapter will analyze the methodology's framework, implementation process, and potential problems during development.

Justification for Selected Methodology
Agile is chosen for implementation into PricePilot as it meets the requirements of the project. One of its strong sides is the ability to cope with the changes of the system's requirements. Contrary to the Waterfall model, where the scope of the system is defined beforehand, in Agile it can change during the whole development period. This is necessary since PricePilot requires the input of user surveys, reviews, and real-life testing, which might differ over time.
While Spiral methodology is also iterative, it is ideal for larger and more risky projects where extensive analysis is required at all stages of development. In our case, adding too much process will only add complexity. The added effort will not provide sufficient value in return. Agile Methodology
Overview
Figure 17Agile Development Lifecycle
Note. From Agile methodology, by InterQuality, n.d. https://interqualitybg.com/en/resources/scrum-and-agile-resources/agile-methodology.
Agile is an adaptable and customer-oriented software development methodology. It uses iterative development cycles known as sprints. In every sprint, an operational and testable part of the system is created  (Vadlamani & Jain, 2025). The development of the entire system is not done in one shot. It is done step by step. Each step helps to improve the previous version.
This methodology relies on the principles of the Agile Manifesto. They include preference for delivering working software, cooperation, and the capacity to adapt to changes  (Hanser, 2026). Planning remains crucial, yet it is flexible. There is constant feedback, and development gradually changes over time.
A typical sprint can be one to four weeks long. At the beginning of every sprint, tasks are chosen from the Product Backlog and development begins. At the end of the sprint, these tasks are fully accomplished and implemented into the system. It brings order to the development process. It also enables better tracking of its results and adjusting when required.
Agile Development Life Cycle Stages
Figure 18Six Stages of Agile Development Life Cycle
Note. From Agile software development life cycle, by Spiceworks, n.d. https://www.spiceworks.com/soft-tech/what-is-agile-software-development/.
The Agile development lifecycle can be described in six phases. All of them are important for ensuring the proper guidance for the project throughout its development. They provide structure without rigidity and allow for flexibility.
Concept
This stage is the beginning of the project. It defines the overall direction and identifies the problem that is being addressed. A prioritized Product Backlog is created to organize system features  (Singh et al., 2026). For PricePilot, this includes real-time price data collection, the Deal Score algorithm, and a mobile-first interface. Features are prioritized so that the most important ones are developed first.
Inception
At this stage, the planning for the first sprint will take place. The backlog items will be translated into user stories, followed by acceptance criteria  (Sharma & Kumar, 2023). Finally, the development environment will be set up. It will consist of version control, cloud services, scraping tool, and backend configuration.
Construction / Iteration
It is the primary and most extensive stage of the Agile development process. Development is done in iterations called sprints that typically last for two weeks. At the end of each sprint, a working and tested component of the system is delivered  (Wakili et al., 2025). In the case of PricePilot, this involves web scraping services, back-end development, implementation of Deal Score, user interface design, and database construction.
Release
Once all features have been implemented and tested after several sprints, the process goes into the release phase. During the release phase, system testing is conducted. This comprises the integration test, user acceptance test, and polishing the system for perfection  (Cherviakov & Kataiev, 2025). It aims to ensure that the system works as intended as a whole product.
Production
Upon the deployment of the system, it goes into the production stage. This stage mainly focuses on performance and reliability maintenance. It monitors the scraping pipeline, tracks API performance, and ensures data accuracy  (Kucharska et al., 2026). Once that is done, the problems are sorted out and added to the Product Backlog.
Retirement
This is the point at which the lifecycle of the system ends. The system will be retired, and all appropriate information will be backed up or transferred  (Sohail et al., 2021). In PricePilot’s case, this represents the completion of the project. The system will be tested to determine its efficiency, and results will be reported.
Activities and Processes in Each Agile Phase
Planning Phase
Planning sets the groundwork for the project. This is accomplished through setting the scope of the system, listing essential elements, and defining the overall architecture. In PricePilot, planning includes the scraping pipeline, price normalization module, Deal Score calculation method, cloud-based database, FastAPI API server, and mobile front-end interface.
           This phase generates the Product Backlog along with user stories for all the items. Features are ranked according to their priority and system needs. This ensures that the most important features such as price comparison in real time, data gathering from various platforms, and the Deal Score are developed first. The cloud architecture is also established with the help of services, databases, and APIs defined during this process.
Requirement Analysis Phase
 In this stage, both functional and non-functional requirements will be determined. For PricePilot, this will be done via a requirement-gathering procedure using a survey of users in Nepal.
           The survey will capture all the behaviors of users, price difficulties faced, and the features expected from various product types. The requirements will then be used to specify the system requirements. These features include price comparison, price history, deal confirmation, Wishlist feature, alert notifications and many more.
Design Phase
In this phase, the requirements are translated to a proper technical design through the utilization of the three-tier architectural approach. The process involves designing the data tier with scraping services. In this case, the process scrapes data from different e-commerce platforms for diverse products. Next, the backend is designed using the FastAPI platform for logic and data handling, whereas the front-end supports user interactions.
           These steps help in creating data flow diagrams, creating database models, and defining APIs to ensure proper communication among the components. The UI wireframe for the proposed system will also be created in order to help define system functionalities.
Implementation Phase
Development is done during this phase by way of sprint cycles. Tasks are chosen from the product backlog, and their status is tracked via a Kanban board (Backlog, In Process, Under Review, Done).
This process requires scraping of modules by making use of Python and Playwright to handle dynamic web pages. The back end is developed using FastAPI while the front end interacts with the API and presents the outcome. GitHub will be used to manage versions as well as test modules prior to integration within the project.
Testing Phase
In this stage, testing is carried out constantly during the development process, as opposed to just performing it at the end of the process. Unit testing and integration testing are employed to confirm that units and various components of the software interact correctly. This is followed by user acceptance testing.
           The system reviews scraping modules for any changes in external platforms. If there are problems, they are added to the Product Backlog for resolution in future sprints. Additionally, it conducts security tests to ensure the safety of stored and transmitted data.
Deployment Phase
This phase adopts an organized approach for deployment. The initial stage involves the use of a staging environment for proper validation before moving into production. The use of Docker ensures consistency between the environments and minimizes deployment errors.
The process involves the use of a CI/CD pipeline via GitHub Actions. Security features like SSL and authentication are incorporated into the system. Once the backend reaches stability, then the mobile app is deployed.
Evaluation and Feedback Phase
In this phase, the system is reviewed based on the aims and performance of the system after it has been deployed. The users will evaluate important aspects like searching for the products, price comparison, Deal Score, and Wishlist feature.
Feedback is collected through this process, and then analyzed to see what areas need improvement. On the basis of that, more items get added to the Product Backlog. Finally, documentation of the entire process is carried out.
Data Gathering Design
Introduction
Research methodology refers to an organized way by which one collects and analyses data to address questions regarding a system under investigation. Methods of research can generally be categorized as qualitative and quantitative. Qualitative methods involve the study of behaviors and attitudes through open-ended questions and interviews. On the other hand, quantitative methods involve collecting numerical and categorical data through questionnaires  (Oh et al., 2026).
It is necessary to carry out research in order to ensure that PricePilot will be designed according to the needs of the actual users, not any assumptions. Although literature and observations show various issues such as the search fatigue, false discounts, and the absence of the possibility to compare, user experience validates their needs, level of trust, and preferred features.
In this project, surveys will be used as the main mode of gathering data. The questionnaire will be sent to online consumers in Nepal, who are currently utilizing local e-commerce sites. Surveys have been chosen as the most appropriate tool for data gathering since they provide efficient ways of collecting data from a wide array of respondents, as well as allowing open-ended questions that provide qualitative data. They are also structured and can be analyzed using the statistical features available in Google Forms.
Advantages of Using a Questionnaire
The questionnaire was chosen to be the main method of collecting data due to the following reasons:
Efficiency: The survey tool covers a lot of respondents without the need to schedule interviews individually. This is critical for a project done in a specific time frame. 
Consistency: Using standard questions ensures that the answers will be given consistently, which allows analyzing them easily. 
Quantitative Insight: Asking structured questions allows measuring data through statistics and graphing.
Qualitative Insight: The open-ended questions allow providing subjective information that cannot be given through multiple choice questions.
Anonymity: Participating anonymously will encourage the respondents to be honest about the misleading pricing practices they encountered. 
Accessibility: Google Form surveys can be accessed by anyone and from any device without logging in.
Types of Questions Used in Survey
There are five categories of questions that have been used in the questionnaire survey, with each playing a certain role in the design of data collection.
Multiple Choice Questions: MCQs are designed in such a way that only one answer can be selected. MCQs are suitable when there is a need for only one correct answer to the proposed question, e.g., demographic questions like age bracket, how often the respondent’s shop, and modes of communication.
Checkbox Questions: Here, the participant is free to select more than one answer if necessary. Such a type of question would be essential if the study seeks information on multiple platforms used and various problems experienced in the process of online shopping.
Likert Scale Questions: These are the types of questions that require a person to rate their opinion regarding something. In simple words, likert scale questions provide the user an opportunity to judge the attitude toward a particular statement on the basis of scale varying from 1 to 5 points.
Ranking Questions: Ranking questions consist of listing various items in order of importance or preference to the respondent. This helps identify what features are important for customers while buying.
Open-ended Questions: In open-ended questions, the respondents are free to express their views without any restriction of response options.
Questionnaire Design
The survey will be developed and distributed through Google Forms. The name of the survey is “PricePilot: Online Shopping and Price Comparison Survey” and it contains 25 questions that are categorized into six sections, where 21 are close-ended questions while four are open-ended questions. Close-ended questions are designed in the form of multiple-choice, checkboxes, Likert scale, and ranking, whereas open-ended questions will give the participants an opportunity to answer freely.
The survey targets online shoppers in Nepal who have purchased or considered purchasing products from local e-commerce platforms. It distributes the survey through social media platforms and direct messaging, aiming to collect at least 50 responses. Once it reaches the target, the survey closes, and the responses are analyzed using Google Forms’ built-in statistical tools.

Summary
In this chapter, the system development methodology used for PricePilot is explained. This begins with the comparison of four system development methodologies which are Waterfall, RAD, Prototyping, and Agile. After thorough evaluation, the Agile methodology is determined to be the best fit for the project due to its ability to allow flexibility, get user feedback continuously, and use a Sprint-based approach which is suitable to a technologically difficult system developed by a single developer. The Agile lifecycle is also described in this chapter as well as the activities involved in each of the six stages, which include creating the product backlog, generating the user stories in planning, developing the scraping system using Python and Playwright in implementation, and deployment using Docker and CI/CD pipelines through GitHub Actions. Lastly, this chapter describes the design of the data gathering process where the online questionnaire survey is used as the main research tool due to its effectiveness in collecting quantitative and qualitative data from Nepali online shoppers.
The survey findings reveal that there is an absolute requirement for PricePilot, based on responses from 30 users. All the respondents are regular online buyers, who use Daraz and other similar platforms for purchasing products. Platforms such as GadgetByte and Evo Store are used by almost half of the respondents. Most of the users usually check two to three platforms before buying a product and spend about 15 to 30 minutes comparing the prices on different platforms. It has been found that several users do not even proceed because of the efforts involved in price comparison. It is also seen that a number of users feel that there might be fake discounts available on these platforms. Moreover, most of them have not got any way of verifying the authenticity of the discounts. It was also found that the key features of PricePilot, such as price comparison and Deal Score, were highly valued by all users, while a majority favored a mobile-first app. Therefore, it can be stated that PricePilot design is in accordance with users' requirements.
Chapter 4: Conclusion
To conclude, this research has been successful in setting up a validated basis for developing PricePilot. This is because it has proven the existence of some actual problems that Nepalese consumers encounter when shopping online, such as fragmentation, searching exhaustion, and unverifiable discounts. Survey results have confirmed the problems and shown a high demand for an easy price-comparison tool.
These issues are solved using PricePilot’s main features such as price gathering, price history monitoring, and the Deal Score that helps to verify discounts. The chosen architecture, technologies, and approach to software development are highly suitable for implementing the solution. The analysis process also contributes to the definition of system requirements by emphasizing the necessity to ensure data transparency and credibility. In general, the project is ready for further development.
Achievements
The present research accomplishes all its major goals and objectives. Firstly, the study manages to detect and confirm several critical issues faced by the majority of Nepali online shoppers. According to the results obtained during the analysis, consumers have difficulties with platform fragmentation, spending considerable amounts of time comparing prices, and the inability to ensure that the discounts they receive are indeed legitimate. Despite the considerable amount of effort put into checking prices on different websites, many buyers are unable to be sure that their prices are accurate. Moreover, the data collected in the survey provides compelling arguments in favor of these claims, and it is evident that there is a growing need for software like PricePilot.
Moreover, the study creates a good base from the technical perspective to proceed with the development process of the system. The chosen architecture, technologies, and Agile framework ensure scalability and future enhancement capabilities. Nevertheless, there are some limitations in terms of research and design. The paper is focused primarily on using survey results as the main source of data without considering any user studies, which might help to understand user interactions better. In addition, it does not focus on more sophisticated aspects such as personalized suggestions and performance testing.
Limitations
There are three primary limitations associated with this study. The first one is that the sample primarily consists of digitally active users because of the nature of its collection through social media and messaging services. Hence, there is a possibility that the results of this survey will not be applicable to other people and people who are not that digitally active but face the same problems. It can also be argued that not all consumers from Nepal will have the same online shopping experience.
Second, the analysis of the system is based on the tools and platforms that existed when the research was done, and these may be subject to changes in the future depending on the development of the market. There may be new tools for comparing prices, as well as new versions of the e-commerce platforms that may alter the structure and affect the longevity of the results found.
Lastly, this research was based exclusively on surveys and did not employ usability tests or interviews, which could have been used to reveal more about the users' interactions with the website.
Reflections
It can be seen from this study that the issue is even more complicated than anticipated earlier. It is because, apart from the need to compare prices, users also need the element of trust in information, particularly in discount information. This helps us understand the significance of providing information about the Deal Score and keeping the information updated, since both these factors contribute significantly to building trust among users.
The research process also helps to enhance important technical and methodological choices. This is because the research proves that it would be better to adopt Agile since there will be constant changes. In addition, the research makes it clear that there will be a need to use PostgreSQL and MongoDB since each of them has its own strengths in handling certain data types. In general, the research helps us gain greater insight into the project and make better choices regarding its further development.
